Fans and evaporative coolers work well up to about 30°C. Above that — or in humid climates where evaporation doesn't help — a portable air conditioner is the only solution that guarantees a productive work environment.

Unlike split systems or window units, portable ACs require no permanent installation. You need one thing: a window or door for the exhaust hose. Beyond that, they're plug-and-play cooling that works in any office or home workspace.

The challenge is finding one that's quiet enough for calls, effective enough for your space, and priced within reach. Here's what we found after testing the top options.

---

## What to look for in a portable AC for office use

**Noise level** is the most critical spec for office workers. Most portable ACs run at 52–60dB — acceptable as consistent background noise, but disruptive on calls if it cycles on and off frequently. Look for units rated below 54dB and with a stable compressor that doesn't produce sudden loud cycles.

**BTU rating** determines room coverage. The rule of thumb:
- 8,000 BTU → up to 200 sq ft (18m²)
- 10,000 BTU → up to 250 sq ft (23m²)
- 12,000 BTU → up to 350 sq ft (32m²)

Don't oversize — a unit too large for the room short-cycles (turns on and off frequently), which is louder and less efficient than a correctly sized unit running continuously.

**Single-hose vs dual-hose** is an important efficiency distinction. Single-hose units draw air from inside the room to cool the compressor, then exhaust it outside — this creates negative pressure that pulls warm air in from other openings. Dual-hose units draw outside air for compressor cooling, making them 30–40% more efficient. For offices with good sealing, single-hose is fine. For open-plan spaces, dual-hose is worth the premium.

**Dehumidifier mode** is valuable in humid climates — high humidity makes heat feel worse even at the same temperature. A portable AC with effective dehumidification addresses both problems simultaneously.

## Installation: what you actually need

A portable AC requires exactly one thing: a path for the exhaust hose to exit the room. Options in order of convenience:

**Window kit (included):** Most portable ACs come with a window kit that fits standard single and double-hung windows. Takes 10–15 minutes to set up. No tools needed.

**Sliding door kit:** Available as an accessory for most units. Fits standard sliding glass doors — useful if your office has patio access but no standard windows.

**Through-wall exhaust:** Possible but requires cutting a hole — not recommended for rented spaces.

**Important:** Never run a portable AC without the exhaust hose connected. The unit will dump heat into the room and raise temperature rather than lower it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How loud is a portable AC for office use?
Most portable ACs run at 52–58dB. The LG LP0821GSSM at 53dB is the quietest in its class — comparable to a quiet conversation or moderate background music. It's audible but consistent, which most people adapt to quickly. Avoid units rated above 58dB for office use.

### Do I need a dual-hose portable AC for an office?
If you run it all day, yes — dual-hose is 30–40% more efficient and maintains temperature more consistently. Single-hose units are fine for occasional use or smaller spaces. For a full workday in a sealed home office, the Whynter dual-hose pays for itself in efficiency within one summer.

### Can I use a portable AC in a rented office?
Yes — portable ACs require only a window opening for the exhaust hose, not permanent modification. Most commercial and residential leases permit this. Confirm with your landlord if uncertain, but it's generally not considered a structural modification.

### What size portable AC do I need for a home office?
8,000 BTU for rooms up to 18m² (200 sq ft). 12,000 BTU for rooms up to 28m² (300 sq ft). 14,000 BTU for rooms up to 37m² (400 sq ft). When in doubt, choose the smaller size — an oversized unit short-cycles and is less efficient than a correctly sized unit running continuously.
